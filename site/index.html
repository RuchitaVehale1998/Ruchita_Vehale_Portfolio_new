Plate II · c. 1011–1021

The dark room that taught light to draw.

MachineCamera obscura
Yearc. 1011–1021
MakerIbn al-Haytham, Book of Optics
Sheet2 / 13

Light entering a small hole projects an inverted image of the world: the pinhole model every camera still uses. Add a second hole and the shift between the two images is depth.

Try: move the red object with the sliders (or drag it), then switch the second pinhole on and read the depth from the disparity.

III

Plate III · 1804

A loom that read holes.

MachineJacquard loom
Year1804
MakerJoseph Marie Jacquard
Sheet3 / 13

A chain of punched cards decides which warp threads rise for each pass of the shuttle. The pattern lives in the cards, not in the loom; Babbage and Hollerith both borrowed the idea.

Try: click holes on any card in the chain to punch or fill them. The cloth is woven from the cards, one row per card, and reads upright.

IV

Plate IV · 18th century

Lost at sea, drawing the coast.

MachineSextant & sea chart
Year18th century
MakerMany hands
Sheet4 / 13

Speed, heading and elapsed time give a position that drifts a little more every hour. A bearing to a known landmark pulls it back. Charting the coast while you steer along it is simultaneous localisation and mapping by another name.

Try: click the water to drop a beacon. The red error ellipse grows between fixes and collapses at each one. Other ships are seen but never charted.

Descendant · MSc dissertation, University of Bristol

Dynamic object-aware visual SLAM

RGB-D and monocular SLAM on Intel RealSense D455, Jetson Orin Nano and iPhone RGB cameras. YOLOv8, DeepSORT and MiDaS find people and estimate depth; RGB-D and estimated-depth dynamic filtering with inpainting removes them before ORB feature matching and pose-graph optimisation handle loop closure and drift. RANSAC pose estimation and SE(3) optimisation produce 2D occupancy grids, 3D point clouds (PLY) and stable trajectories.

Plate V · 1852 – 1910s

A spinning wheel that refuses to fall.

MachineGyroscope & gyro-stabiliser
Year1852 · Sperry, 1910s
MakerFoucault · Sperry
Sheet5 / 13

A fast rotor keeps its axis while everything around it tilts. Foucault named it in 1852; Sperry's stabilisers steered ships and aircraft in the 1910s. It is the idea inside every flight controller's attitude estimate.

Try: tilt the frame with the sliders or by dragging sideways. The rotor holds still; the horizon instrument follows the frame.

Descendant · HVN Labs, Future Space, BRL · Jr. Drone System Engineer, Jun – Aug 2025

Autonomous precision landing, no GPS

An upward-facing Raspberry Pi camera on the landing pad detects the incoming drone with YOLOv8 and tracks it with a custom DeepSORT: Kalman filter, Hungarian assignment and CNN appearance embeddings, plus stereo-vision depth to recover metric 3D position. Corrective MAVLink LANDING_TARGET messages stream at 10 Hz to a Cube Orange (ArduCopter) for closed-loop landing. The detector is pluggable across four modes (motion, brightness, YOLOv8, YOLOv8 + DeepSORT). Earlier in 2025, as Technology Intern: a coaxial drone for synchronised lighting displays, from ESC calibration to flight testing.

VIII

Plate VIII · 1951 – 1953

Bits as magnets, threaded on wire.

MachineMagnetic-core memory
Year1951 – 1953
MakerJay Forrester, MIT
Sheet8 / 13

Each bit is a tiny ferrite ring. Two half-currents flip one ring and leave the rest alone. Reading erases the bit, so every read is followed by a write-back. It first ran in MIT's Whirlwind in 1953.

Try: type into memory (two bytes per row, ASCII), or click any core to flip it.

Type a message. Enigma does the rest.

MachineEnigma I
Year1918 patent · WWII service
MakerArthur Scherbius
Sheet13 / 13

Three rotors step like an odometer, so every key press is enciphered differently, and enciphering twice returns the text. No letter ever maps to itself, the flaw Bletchley Park leaned on.

Try: type letters into the field, or click the lamp keyboard. The red path is the current through the rotors.
